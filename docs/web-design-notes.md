# Web design basics, and how to ask for changes

These notes are for you, the coach. They have two parts: a few web design basics,
and a simple way to ask for changes so you get what you picture the first time.

## Part 1: Web design basics

### 1. Hierarchy: what people see first, second, third

Every section has one main thing, usually the heading. Size, weight and colour
create the order. On your site the big serif "Tennis lessons near Kovan" comes
first, the grey line under it second, then the green WhatsApp button.

### 2. Layout and grid

Content sits in columns of equal width. When a page looks "untidy", something has
usually broken the grid. Five cards in a three-column grid leave an empty slot,
which is why the "Lessons for every level" cards looked messy until the last row
was centred.

### 3. Whitespace (spacing)

The empty space around things. Too little feels cramped, and too much feels empty,
like the green strip on the left of the top section on a big monitor. Keeping the
gaps between sections, and between cards, the same is what makes a page feel neat.

### 4. Alignment

Things line up on invisible lines: left edges match, and centred things share a
centre. Mixed alignment, such as a centred heading over left-aligned cards, looks
careless.

### 5. Colour

- One main brand colour (your green).
- One accent (the tennis-ball yellow).
- Neutrals: white, pale green, grey text.
- A dark colour for headings and body text.

Text needs enough contrast against its background to be readable. That is why the
yellow is only used on dark green, never on white.

### 6. Typography

Use at most two fonts: one for headings (your serif) and one for body text. Keep
clear size steps: heading, then subheading, then body.

### 7. Responsive design

The same page has to work on a phone (about 390px wide), a tablet (about 768px), a
laptop (about 1280px) and a big monitor (about 1900px). A page can look fine at one
width and broken at another. The empty left side only showed up on wide screens.

### 8. Images

Real, sharp photos beat any design trick. A photo can:

- sit behind the text (a "hero background", like your top section),
- sit inside a rounded box (a "card"),
- or sit in a row with others (a "gallery", like the three photos in "Meet your
  coach").

## Part 2: How to ask for a change

What helps most:

1. **Say what is wrong, not just that it is wrong.** "Too much empty space on the
   left on my big monitor" is much easier to act on than "looks weird".
2. **Say what to keep.** "Keep the photo blending into the green, just fill the left
   side." One sentence like this saves a whole round of changes.
3. **Say which screen size.** Phone, laptop or big monitor. A screenshot shows the
   width too, so keep sending them.
4. **Point at the exact spot.** Circling it, like you have been doing, works well.
5. **Show an example you like.** A screenshot of another site, with "make it feel
   like this".
6. **Say whether it is a small tweak or a new layout.** "Slightly bigger" and
   "change the layout" lead to very different work.

### A template you can copy

> **Section:** (for example the top of the page, Meet your coach, the price cards)
> **Screen:** (phone, laptop or big monitor)
> **Problem:** (what looks wrong, in your own words)
> **Keep:** (what you like and do not want changed)
> **Want:** (what it should look like, or a screenshot of an example)
> **Size of change:** (small tweak or new layout)

### Example

> Section: top of the home page. Screen: big monitor. Problem: wide empty green
> strip on the left. Keep: the photo fading into the green. Want: the text to start
> closer to the left edge and fill the space. Size: small tweak.

## Useful words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| hero | The big top section with the main heading |
| full-bleed | Goes edge to edge, with no margins |
| card | A box with a border or shadow holding one item |
| grid / columns | How many items sit side by side |
| padding / spacing | The empty space inside or between things |
| align left / centre | Which edge things line up on |
| above the fold | What you can see before scrolling |
| overlay / gradient | A see-through colour layer over a photo |
| CTA | Call-to-action button (your WhatsApp button) |
